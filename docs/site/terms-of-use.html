What PatternVault provides

PatternVault stores photos, videos, memos, and documents in separate local vaults opened with patterns. Optional functions include biometric access, encrypted backup files, iCloud backup, and read-only sharing. The current app requires iOS or iPadOS 26.2 or later; feature availability also depends on compatible hardware, permissions, storage, and Apple services.

The free version allows three owned vaults and five total items in each vault, including items in folders and all supported item types. Received shared vaults do not count toward the owned-vault limits. PatternVault Pro removes these app limits and enables creating shares; device capacity and iCloud storage limits still apply.

Patterns, recovery, and backups

You are responsible for protecting your device, Apple Account, patterns, recovery phrases, and sharing phrases. Keep recovery information outside PatternVault in a place you can access if the app or device is lost.

Local media uses iOS file protection and an app access lock, not media encryption derived from your pattern. Backup archives are encrypted separately. Security features reduce particular risks; they do not promise absolute secrecy, screenshot prevention, or protection on a compromised device.

A recovery phrase alone cannot recreate deleted files. Restoration needs a completed encrypted cloud backup or an exported backup file and the phrase valid for that backup. Local vaults are excluded from ordinary device backups. Verify a completed backup before deleting PatternVault, resetting a device, or changing devices. We cannot recover lost secrets or content for which no usable copy exists.

Cloud services and sharing

iCloud backup and sharing depend on Apple, connectivity, the correct Apple Account, and available storage. Apple’s Privacy Policy and iCloud terms also apply. PatternVault’s backup runs while the vault is open, retains the latest completed version, and is not continuous background or two-way device synchronization.

Each sharing phrase creates a claim for one recipient Apple Account. The recipient’s vault is read-only and requires online access checks. Expiration, per-installation open limits, export controls, and capture-triggered locking cannot guarantee that content has not been copied. Revocation needs to reach iCloud and cannot erase files already exported, screenshots already taken, or external photographs.

Deleting the app is not a request to delete all cloud content. Follow the removal controls in the app and let pending requests complete before uninstalling.

Duress and irreversible actions

Duress is an advanced, consequential feature. Enabling it arms the current vault’s pattern. Unlocking with that armed pattern keeps the selected dummy vault, or the triggering vault if there is no dummy, and removes access credentials for every other local vault. It generates a new recovery phrase for the surviving vault, stops backups and sharing on that installation, and queues owned-share revocations.

Other vault files remain on disk, but their previous patterns and phrases no longer open them in PatternVault. Turning duress off does not undo this. A separately saved, usable encrypted backup may be restored explicitly. Duress is not cryptographic erasure, an emergency service, or a guarantee of concealment or personal safety. A failed Face ID or Touch ID check that opens a dummy vault does not itself activate duress.

Deleting items or a vault also has consequences described in the app. Read confirmations before proceeding and keep independent backups where needed.
